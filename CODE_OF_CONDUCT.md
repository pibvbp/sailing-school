# Code of conduct

Sailing School is a place to learn and to build something together. People come here with every level of experience,
on the water and in code. We want each of them to feel welcome.

## What we expect

- **Be kind.** Assume good intent, and remember there is a person behind every username. Beginners' questions are
  welcome; everyone started somewhere.
- **Be constructive.** Criticise ideas and code, not people. When you disagree, explain why and suggest something
  better. When you are wrong, say so and move on.
- **Be inclusive.** Welcome people whatever their background, identity, age, ability, experience or nationality.
  Write plainly, so that non-native English speakers and newcomers can follow.
- **Respect people's time and boundaries.** Stay on topic, and take "no" for an answer.

## What we don't accept

- Harassment of any kind, in public or in private.
- Insults, slurs, personal attacks, and demeaning jokes about who someone is.
- Sexual language or imagery, and unwelcome attention.
- Threats, and encouraging anyone to harm themselves or others.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission.
- Trolling, and deliberately derailing conversations.

This applies in the project's issues, pull requests and other spaces, and whenever someone represents the project
elsewhere.

## Reporting a problem

If someone's behaviour breaks this code, or makes you feel unsafe or unwelcome, please tell the maintainer privately.

- **Use the repository's private report form:** the **Security** tab, then **Report a vulnerability**
  (<https://github.com/pibvbp/sailing-school/security/advisories/new>). It isn't only for security bugs: start the title
  with "Conduct report". Only the maintainers can see what you send.
- **Report content to GitHub** if it breaks GitHub's own rules: use the "…" menu on any comment, issue or pull request.

Reports are handled in confidence, and we won't share who made one. You will get a reply, and nobody will be
penalised for making a report in good faith.

## What happens next

The maintainers decide what to do. The response depends on the situation: it can be a private word, a public
reminder, removing or editing comments, locking a thread, or a temporary or permanent ban from the project.
